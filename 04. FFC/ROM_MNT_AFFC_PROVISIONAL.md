# 1. Cersei's Struggle to Dominate the Regency and the Tyrell Alliance

<table>
<thead><tr><th width="50%"><h2>CERSEI'S EXCLUSIVE ROYAL CONTROL</h2></th><th width="50%"><h2>TYRELL DYNASTIC INFLUENCE</h2></th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>CERSEI LANNISTER</strong> — Asserts personal control over Tommen's government.</td><td><strong>MARGAERY TYRELL</strong> — Protects her status as Tommen's queen.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>TOMMEN BARATHEON</strong> — Child king whose authority Cersei exercises; not an independent political partner.</td><td><strong>MACE TYRELL</strong> — Seeks positions and influence for his family.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>QYBURN</strong> — Serves Cersei as an adviser and clandestine agent.</td><td><strong>LORAS TYRELL</strong> — Advances Tyrell interests through royal military service.</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

After **Tywin Lannister's** death, **Cersei Lannister** wants to govern through her young son **Tommen** without surrendering authority to her uncle **Kevan** or the politically powerful **Tyrells**. Her fear that **Margaery Tyrell** will displace her, intensified by an old prophecy, turns a necessary dynastic alliance into a perceived struggle for survival.

Cersei refuses Kevan's conditions for serving as Hand, appoints compliant councillors and attempts to marginalise **Mace Tyrell**. She also delays substantial assistance against the ironborn attacks on the Reach and permits **Loras Tyrell** to lead the dangerous assault on **Dragonstone**. Her eventual plot to accuse Margaery of adultery relies on **Osney Kettleblack** and the religious authorities she has empowered.

The attempted prosecution collapses when Osney implicates Cersei in crimes of her own. Both queens are arrested by the Faith, while Kevan returns to govern and **Aurane Waters** escapes with the crown's new warships. By the book's end Cersei's pursuit of unshared authority has crippled her own position and destabilised the government she meant to secure.

# 2. Euron's Seizure of the Iron Islands and Expansion Against the Reach

<table>
<thead><tr><th width="33%"><h2>EURON'S CONQUEST</h2></th><th width="34%"><h2>VICTARION'S COMPETING AMBITION</h2></th><th width="33%"><h2>ASHA'S ALTERNATIVE CLAIM</h2></th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>EURON GREYJOY</strong> — Wins the kingsmoot and promises conquest through dragons.</td><td><strong>VICTARION GREYJOY</strong> — First seeks the crown; later obeys Euron while planning a private betrayal.</td><td><strong>ASHA GREYJOY</strong> — Competes for the Seastone Chair and proposes a different course.</td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><strong>AERON GREYJOY</strong> — Initially backs Victarion to stop Euron, then seeks resistance to his rule.</td><td></td></tr>
</tbody></table>

The death of **Balon Greyjoy** leaves the **Iron Islands** without an uncontested ruler. **Euron Greyjoy** wants a far greater dominion than Balon's failed northern conquest; **Victarion** and **Asha** advance rival claims, while **Aeron** fears that Euron's impiety and ruthlessness make him unfit to rule. Their contest concerns both succession and the future direction of the ironborn.

At the kingsmoot on **Old Wyk**, Euron promises that a magical horn and the dragons of **Daenerys Targaryen** can enable the ironborn to conquer Westeros. He wins the crown and launches raids against the **Reach**, taking the **Shield Islands** and putting **Oldtown's** approaches in danger. The attacks create a new military crisis while much of the realm assumes the preceding war is ending.

Euron's commanders become less enthusiastic about an eastern expedition, so he sends Victarion towards Daenerys while he maintains his position at home. Victarion intends to take Daenerys for himself rather than faithfully serve his brother. Euron has established a fresh expansionist power, but the rulers and commanders beneath him remain divided over its objectives.

# 3. The Faith Militant's Restoration and Challenge to Royal Authority

<table>
<thead><tr><th width="50%"><h2>THE FAITH'S INDEPENDENT AUTHORITY</h2></th><th width="50%"><h2>ROYAL CONTROL AND ITS VULNERABLE COURT</h2></th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>HIGH SPARROW</strong> — Builds popular authority and restores the Faith's armed orders.</td><td><strong>CERSEI LANNISTER</strong> — Initially empowers the Faith for political advantage, then falls under its jurisdiction.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>LANCEL LANNISTER</strong> — Renounces secular advancement for religious service.</td><td><strong>MARGAERY TYRELL</strong> — Becomes the target of a royal accusation handled by the Faith.</td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><strong>OSNEY KETTLEBLACK</strong> — Recruited for Cersei's accusation; his confession exposes her involvement.</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

The war has displaced impoverished and devout people across **Westeros**, helping the Sparrows become a mass religious movement. Their leader, the **High Sparrow**, seeks moral discipline and institutional independence for the **Faith of the Seven**. The crown's indebtedness gives him leverage, while **Cersei Lannister** mistakes a concession to his demands for a convenient instrument against her rivals.

In exchange for relief from royal debts and cooperation over **Tommen's** kingship, Cersei restores the Faith Militant's long-forbidden armed orders. The High Sparrow gains coercive power that does not depend on the royal court. Cersei then attempts to employ his authority against **Margaery Tyrell**, constructing an accusation of adultery through **Osney Kettleblack**.

The High Sparrow's investigation is not confined to the victim Cersei selected. Osney's confession incriminates her, and the Faith imprisons both queens, demonstrating that its new authority can directly challenge the monarchy. The religious revival has thus become a distinct, armed political force rather than merely another faction available for court intrigue.

# 4. Dornish Revenge, the Myrcella Coup and Doran's Targaryen Design

<table>
<thead><tr><th width="33%"><h2>DORAN'S CONCEALED PLAN</h2></th><th width="34%"><h2>ARIANNE'S IMMEDIATE COUP</h2></th><th width="33%"><h2>SAND SNAKES' RETALIATION</h2></th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>DORAN MARTELL</strong> — Restrains open war while pursuing a hidden Targaryen restoration scheme.</td><td><strong>ARIANNE MARTELL</strong> — Tries to crown Myrcella and believes her inheritance is threatened.</td><td><strong>OBARA SAND</strong> — Demands armed reprisal for Oberyn's death.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>AREO HOTAH</strong> — Enforces Doran's authority and stops the coup.</td><td><strong>ARYS OAKHEART</strong> — Helps Arianne before being killed during the interception.</td><td><strong>NYMERIA SAND</strong> — Advocates retaliatory action by a different means.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>QUENTYN MARTELL</strong> — Undertakes a confidential mission east; details remain outside his POV.</td><td><strong>MYRCELLA BARATHEON</strong> — Unwilling dynastic claimant central to the proposed coronation.</td><td></td></tr>
</tbody></table>

The death of **Oberyn Martell** intensifies demands for revenge against the **Lannisters**, but **Doran Martell** fears that an immediate war would imperil **Dorne**. His daughter **Arianne** mistakes his secrecy for weakness and suspects that he intends to disinherit her in favour of **Quentyn**. The **Sand Snakes** also demand action, though their proposed forms of retaliation differ.

Arianne attempts to exploit Dornish succession law by crowning **Myrcella Baratheon** ahead of **Tommen**, hoping to force confrontation with the Iron Throne. **Areo Hotah** intercepts the conspirators; **Arys Oakheart** is killed and Myrcella is wounded. Doran imprisons Arianne and restrains the Sand Snakes, preventing their separate initiatives from immediately committing Dorne to war.

Doran finally reveals that he had intended Arianne to marry **Viserys Targaryen**, placing her on the Iron Throne, and that Quentyn has now gone east on another mission. His motives were neither loyalty to the Lannisters nor simple inaction: he has pursued a slower dynastic strategy. By the book's end, the concealed scheme turns towards **Daenerys**, whose response remains unknown.

# 5. Riverrun's Surrender and the Unfinished Pacification of the Riverlands

<table>
<thead><tr><th width="50%"><h2>LANNISTER–FREY SETTLEMENT</h2></th><th width="50%"><h2>TULLY RESISTANCE AND SURVIVAL</h2></th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>JAIME LANNISTER</strong> — Directs the reduction of Riverrun while seeking a surrender without battle.</td><td><strong>BRYNDEN TULLY</strong> — Defies the besiegers and preserves a route of escape.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>RYMAN FREY</strong> — Participates in the siege and threatens the captive Edmure.</td><td><strong>EDMURE TULLY</strong> — Surrenders his castle under coercion but helps Brynden escape.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>EMMON FREY</strong> — Becomes the installed lord of Riverrun.</td><td></td></tr>
</tbody></table>

The victorious **Lannister–Frey** regime needs to turn its military gains into durable control over the ruined **Riverlands**. **Jaime Lannister** is sent to end the siege of **Riverrun**, where **Brynden Tully** refuses submission. Jaime seeks to prevent another costly assault, yet his coercive authority depends on the same threats and political bargains that have left the region embittered.

Jaime negotiates with the captive **Edmure Tully**, whose legal position as lord enables a surrender that Brynden will not accept. He threatens Edmure's family to compel compliance. Edmure ultimately yields Riverrun but deliberately delays the handover, giving Brynden the opportunity to escape through the castle's waterways.

**Emmon Frey** receives Riverrun, while Brynden remains at large. Jaime's journey exposes devastation, hunger, war crimes and the weakness of a victory that has not restored ordinary life. As snow begins falling, the apparent end of organised warfare gives way to a harsher question: whether the new order can survive widespread resentment and the approach of winter.

# 6. Brienne's Search for the Stark Girls and Stoneheart's Vengeance

<table>
<thead><tr><th width="50%"><h2>BRIENNE'S PROTECTIVE OATH</h2></th><th width="50%"><h2>STONEHEART'S DEMAND FOR RETRIBUTION</h2></th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>BRIENNE OF TARTH</strong> — Searches for Sansa and Arya to fulfil her promises.</td><td><strong>LADY STONEHEART</strong> — Judges Brienne by her failure and demands Jaime's death.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>PODRICK PAYNE</strong> — Accompanies Brienne and is endangered by Stoneheart's sentence.</td><td><strong>LEM LEMONCLOAK</strong> — Member of the Brotherhood carrying out Stoneheart's authority.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>HYLE HUNT</strong> — Joins the journey and shares its final peril.</td><td><strong>THOROS OF MYR</strong> — Explains the Brotherhood's changed leadership.</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

**Brienne of Tarth** wants to honour her promises to **Catelyn Stark** and **Jaime Lannister** by finding the missing **Stark** daughters. She has little reliable information, no political protection and limited means of distinguishing rumours from fact. Her search carries her through communities whose suffering shows how little peace the great lords' victories have brought.

Travelling with **Podrick Payne** and later **Hyle Hunt**, Brienne follows leads across **Duskendale**, **Crackclaw Point**, the **Quiet Isle** and the Riverlands. She confronts surviving Bloody Mummers and protects vulnerable travellers, but discovers neither **Sansa** nor **Arya**. Her own fidelity contrasts with the widespread opportunism and brutality around her.

The remnants of the Brotherhood without Banners take Brienne before the resurrected Catelyn, now **Lady Stoneheart**. Stoneheart regards Brienne's failure to return with her daughters as betrayal and orders her to kill Jaime; when Brienne refuses, the captives are hanged. Brienne shouts a word as the nooses tighten, leaving the result unresolved at the book's boundary.

# 7. Littlefinger's Hold on the Vale and Sansa's Concealed Inheritance

<table>
<thead><tr><th width="50%"><h2>BAELISH'S REGENCY AND DYNASTIC DESIGN</h2></th><th width="50%"><h2>THE LORDS DECLARANT'S RESISTANCE</h2></th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>PETYR BAELISH</strong> — Protects his position as Robert Arryn's regent and plans a Stark restoration through marriage.</td><td><strong>YOHN ROYCE</strong> — Principal opponent questioning Baelish's authority in the Vale.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>SANSA STARK</strong> — Lives as Alayne Stone, dependent on Baelish and integral to his proposed marriage plan.</td><td></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>LYN CORBRAY</strong> — Secretly plays a disruptive role for Baelish during the confrontation.</td><td></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>ROBERT ARRYN</strong> — Young lord whose guardianship confers power; not a voluntary political partner.</td><td></td></tr>
</tbody></table>

**Petyr Baelish** wants to retain control of the **Vale** after **Lysa Arryn's** death, while **Sansa Stark** needs concealment from enemies who could exploit her identity. The **Lords Declarant** dispute Petyr's authority over young **Robert Arryn**. Petyr's guardianship of Sansa serves both her immediate survival and his own longer political ambitions.

When Vale lords confront Petyr, he manipulates their negotiations with the assistance of **Lyn Corbray**, whose apparently hostile conduct gives Petyr leverage. He secures a year in which to govern without their interference. Sansa, passing as Alayne Stone, observes how his manoeuvres depend on timing, reputation and other people's assumptions.

Petyr then describes a possible marriage between Sansa and **Harrold Hardyng**, who could inherit the Vale should Robert die. Such a match might eventually give Sansa a force capable of reclaiming **Winterfell**, but the proposal is not an accomplished alliance. As the household descends from the **Eyrie** for winter, Sansa remains hidden and dependent upon her guardian's calculations.

# 8. Samwell's Mission to the Citadel and the Warning of Returning Magic

| Character | Role in MNT |
|---|---|
| **SAMWELL TARLY** | Principal Driver; carries the Watch's need for knowledge and Aemon's warning to Oldtown. |
| **AEMON TARGARYEN** | Interprets news of Daenerys and charges Sam to seek assistance before dying. |
| **GILLY** | Travels with Sam and bears the painful consequence of the baby exchange. |
| **MARWYN** | Reveals the Citadel's conflict over magic and departs to seek Daenerys. |
| **ALLERAS** | Helps receive Sam at the Citadel. |
| **DAREON** | Deserts his duties, worsening the group's difficulties in Braavos. |

**Samwell Tarly** is sent by **Jon Snow** from the **Wall** to the Citadel in **Oldtown**, where he must train and seek knowledge about the **Others**. He escorts the ageing **Aemon Targaryen** and **Gilly**, whose grief is linked to the exchange of her baby for **Mance Rayder's** child. The Watch's ignorance of its supernatural enemy makes the mission more than routine study.

Illness, delay and **Dareon's** desertion disrupt the journey through **Braavos**. Reports of **Daenerys Targaryen's** living dragons lead Aemon to reconsider the promised saviour prophecy and hope that Daenerys may be its subject. He dies before reaching Oldtown, leaving Sam with instructions to make the Citadel understand the significance of these reports.

In Oldtown, **Marwyn** warns that influential maesters resist the resurgence of magic and reveals the renewed use of glass candles. He leaves for Daenerys while Sam prepares to study. The apparent reappearance of the novice **Pate**, who died after surrendering a stolen key in the Prologue, adds a separate unresolved danger inside the Citadel.

# 9. Arya's Training with the Faceless Men and the Struggle to Abandon Her Identity

| Character | Role in MNT |
|---|---|
| **ARYA STARK** | Principal Driver; seeks a new life and abilities while retaining unresolved Stark loyalties. |
| **KINDLY MAN** | Teaches the demands and discipline of the Faceless Men. |
| **THE WAIF** | Participates in Arya's training at the House of Black and White. |
| **DAREON** | His desertion prompts Arya to act according to her own judgment. |

**Arya Stark** arrives in **Braavos** with her family scattered and her former life increasingly difficult to recover. The **House of Black and White** offers instruction, shelter and a new identity, but acceptance requires her to surrender the personal attachments and private judgments that still shape her actions.

Arya learns the rituals and disciplines of the **Faceless Men** and lives among the city's people as Cat of the Canals. Yet she hides **Needle** rather than discard it, preserving a tangible link to her Stark past. Her encounter with **Samwell Tarly** and the deserter **Dareon** reconnects her with the world she ostensibly intends to leave behind.

Arya kills Dareon for abandoning the **Night's Watch**, an act arising from her own moral judgment rather than an explicitly assigned killing. After she reports this to the House, she awakens blind. Whether the condition chiefly represents discipline, punishment or another stage of training remains unresolved within the authorised summary evidence.

**Preliminary assessment:** The book's most consequential connected developments concern the fragmentation of authority after the War of the Five Kings: the regency's self-inflicted crisis, the Faith's independent militarisation, fresh Greyjoy expansion, Dornish plotting and the coercive settlement of the Riverlands. Brienne, Sansa, Samwell and Arya reveal different forms of survival and redirection within that changing political world.
