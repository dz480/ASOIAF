# 1. Narrative Context

The long summer has entered its ninth year. At **Winterfell**, **Bran Stark**, the young son of **Eddard Stark**, looks forward to becoming a knight and is fascinated by climbing the castle's walls and towers. His father rules the North and expects his sons to learn the burdens of justice. Meanwhile, stories from beyond **the Wall** trouble the edges of Bran's childhood: **Old Nan** speaks of the Others and the Long Night, while the Night's Watch still guards against threats in the far North. Bran begins among his family, with freedom of movement and an apparently secure place in his father's household, unaware of the changes about to overtake both.

# 4. Narrative Overview


**Bran Stark's** story begins on a clear, cold morning when his father **Eddard** takes him to witness the execution of a deserter from the Night's Watch. Bran is old enough to join **Robb**, **Jon Snow** and the men on the ride, but the sight of death disturbs him. Eddard later explains that a ruler who passes judgment ought to wield the sword. On the journey home, the party discovers a dead direwolf with surviving pups. The children are allowed to keep them, and Bran comes to call his own wolf **Summer**. The episode places family obligation, mortality and the bond with the direwolves at the beginning of his personal narrative.

The royal visit to **Winterfell** has made Bran's future seem suddenly expansive. He expects to travel to **King's Landing** with his father and sisters. Before departure, however, his love of climbing leads him to a disused tower, where he discovers **Cersei** and **Jaime Lannister** in an intimate encounter. Jaime catches him at the window and then deliberately pushes him into the open air. The fall ends Bran's childhood freedom and leaves the truth of the assault hidden from him.

Bran's long unconsciousness is punctuated by a dream of plunging downward while a **three-eyed crow** urges him to fly. Vast regions of Westeros, members of his family and the cold darkness beyond the Wall enter this visionary landscape. Bran finally awakens with Summer nearby. He has survived but can no longer move his legs, and the circumstances that brought him to the tower have disappeared from his conscious memory.

Back at Winterfell, Bran's enforced dependence makes his former hopes of knighthood painful to contemplate. The departures of his father, mother and siblings have altered the household around him, while Robb shoulders new duties. **Old Nan's** tales of the Long Night and the Others continue to exert their pull. When **Tyrion Lannister** returns from the Wall, his plans for an adapted saddle offer Bran something practical that had seemed lost: the chance to ride again. Bran's initial resentment slowly gives way to anticipation.

His return to horseback takes him into the **Wolfswood**, where reports of violence against Stark men in King's Landing intrude on his recovered freedom. A group of southbound fugitives ambushes him. **Robb**, **Theon Greyjoy**, Summer and **Grey Wind** intervene, killing several attackers; Theon shoots **Stiv** before the man can kill Bran. The surviving wildling **Osha** is brought to Winterfell. The confrontation confirms both Bran's exposure to physical danger and the ferocity of his wolf's protection.

As the Lannister conflict escalates, Winterfell becomes a gathering place for northern forces. Bran watches Robb assert his authority over formidable bannermen, including **Greatjon Umber**, and begins to recognise the demands of Stark lordship from a position very different from the one he once imagined. Osha, now serving in the household, warns that the greater danger lies beyond the Wall. **Maester Luwin** rejects much of her account as superstition, but the warnings resonate with Bran's dreams and Old Nan's stories. Robb leads his army south, leaving Bran and the much younger **Rickon** in Winterfell.

After the departure, Bran's sense of connection to unseen events grows more unsettling. He dreams of his father in the crypts, and Rickon has experienced a similarly troubling dream. Amid a discussion of the children of the forest and the persistence of old magic, a wounded raven arrives bearing a message. Luwin's reaction confirms what the children already dread: Eddard is dead. The boy introduced through his father's uncompromising act of justice now faces that father's loss, confined to Winterfell yet increasingly responsible for the home and family that remain.
