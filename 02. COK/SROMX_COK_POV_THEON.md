# 1. Narrative Context

**Theon Greyjoy** returns to the **Iron Islands** for the first time in 10 years, entrusted by **Robb Stark** with an alliance proposal against the **Lannisters**. Since **Balon Greyjoy’s** defeated rebellion, Theon has been **Eddard Stark’s** hostage and ward at **Winterfell**, raised alongside Robb but conscious of his own claim to **Pyke**. He hopes to return as a trusted Greyjoy heir and use his friendship with Robb to win an independent crown for his father. Yet Balon has neither forgiven the Starks nor forgotten the old ironborn ways, leaving Theon caught between the family who raised him and the family whose approval he longs to regain.

# 4. Narrative Overview

**Theon Greyjoy** returns to **Pyke** aboard the *Myraham*, expecting recognition as his father’s heir and bearer of **Robb Stark’s** alliance proposal. His uncle **Aeron Damphair** greets him coldly and insists on the rites of the **Drowned God**. **Balon Greyjoy**, gaunt and embittered, condemns the son he believes the Starks have made soft, tears his gold chain away and burns Robb’s letter. Balon will pay the iron price for a crown, not accept a kingship granted by someone else.

At **Lordsport**, Theon courts an audacious young woman calling herself **Esgred**, boasting of his ship, inheritance and prospective command. She knows more of the ironborn than he does. At the family feast she reveals herself as his sister **Asha**, publicly mocking him before Balon and his uncles. Balon then outlines an invasion of **the North**. Theon receives only 8 longships to harry the **Stony Shore**, while Asha is assigned **Deepwood Motte** and **Victarion** commands the assault on **Moat Cailin**.

On the Stony Shore, Theon’s men destroy a fishing village and rout **Benfred Tallhart’s** Wild Hares. Benfred spits at his former Stark companion and is dragged away for a ritual drowning under Aeron. Attempting to maintain discipline, Theon shoots the disorderly looter **Todric**, who is subsequently killed by other ironborn. His childhood tutor **Dagmer Cleftjaw** provides more respect than Balon ever has, and Theon persuades him to attack **Torrhen’s Square**. The attack is a diversion: Theon intends to seize the lightly defended Winterfell while its forces ride away.

The seizure itself falls between Theon’s selected POV chapters. When his narration resumes, he sleeps in **Eddard Stark’s** former chambers with **Kyra** and governs the occupied castle. He discovers that **Bran**, **Rickon**, their companions and the direwolves have escaped, leaving 2 dead ironborn guards. With **Maester Luwin**, hunters, hounds and the captive **Reek**, he searches the **Wolfswood**. Their trail disappears into running water. As night approaches, Reek suggests an isolated mill and reveals a wolf-head brooch. Rather than confess that the Stark boys have escaped, Theon resolves upon a substitution.

By his next chapter, 2 sons of the miller have been killed, mutilated and passed off as Bran and Rickon, their tarred heads displayed above Winterfell’s gates. Theon suffers nightmares in which the children and other dead figures pursue him. He remembers the horror of the household, unexplained deaths among his own soldiers and his clumsy execution of kennelmaster **Farlen**. **Asha** arrives with too few men to secure the castle. She argues that the ironborn cannot sustain so distant an inland conquest and urges him to abandon it, but Theon refuses to surrender the title he has claimed. Reek offers to obtain 200 fighting men in return for silver and the kennel girl **Palla**; Theon accepts.

**Rodrik Cassel** arrives with a northern army. Theon has only a small garrison and orders **Beth Cassel** threatened with a noose so that her father will hesitate. During the parley, Rodrik denounces his former pupil and demands Winterfell’s surrender. Theon recognises that his hostage may not deter an assault, and **Luwin** offers him an alternative: surrender and join the **Night’s Watch**. Before he can commit, a force bearing **House Bolton’s** flayed man strikes Rodrik’s army from behind and destroys it. Theon opens Winterfell’s gates to his apparent rescuers. Their captain removes a red helm to reveal Reek, who now explains that he is actually **Ramsay Snow**, disguised after exchanging clothes with the original servant. Ramsay strikes Theon in the face and attacks the ironborn garrison. Theon’s last conscious sight is of his horse **Smiler** escaping burning stables, mane ablaze.

Theon’s chapters are 11, 24, 37, 50, 56 and 66, corresponding to EPUB source files part0018.xhtml, part0031.xhtml, part0044.xhtml, part0057.xhtml, part0063.xhtml and part0073.xhtml. Source counts total **32,000 words**. The current SROMX scaling formula gives approximately **2,450 characters** for Narrative Overview and **613 characters** for Narrative Context, used as targets rather than ceilings.

> **POV integrity and identity — Applies to: Entire document:** The capture of Winterfell is narrated in another POV and referenced here only as context between Theon’s Chapters 37 and 50. The woman using **Esgred** is **Asha Greyjoy**, not another character. The apparent servant **Reek** is **Ramsay Snow**, whose identity and exchange of clothing with the original Reek become explicit in **Theon VI**. The true **Bran** and **Rickon** are not physically present in Theon’s six selected chapters; their reported escape and the false deaths of two miller’s sons must remain separate. Dreamed, reported and remembered appearances of other characters do not supply direct enacted physical Presence.

> **Chronology — Applies to: Opening header and Section 2:** The existing [COK CSRM](ROM_CSRM_ACOK.md) uses approximate calendar dates inferred from PrivateMajor’s ASOIAF fan timeline. Day-level placement is not official George R. R. Martin canon. Dates are therefore marked estimated. The ASOIAF Project has not authorised a Reader-Standardised elapsed-time anchor for this Scope; none is invented.

> **Roll Call and deaths — Applies to: Section 5:** Existing significance ranks follow the priority-verified [COK MCM](ROM_MCM_COK.md), with source-grounded Tertiary representation for individually useful participants. The records distinguish the murder of the 2 miller’s children from the supposed deaths of Bran and Rickon. Identified casualties include Benfred, Todric, the 2 substitute boys, Drennan, Squint, Aggar, Farlen, Rodrik, Cley, Leobald, Red Rolfe and Kenned. The additional dead in the coastal raids and the fight outside Winterfell make the exact total unknowable from the narrated chapters. **Theon’s** closing fate, **Luwin’s** fate during the assault and several threatened garrison members remain explicitly unresolved within the final selected chapter, regardless of later evidence.
