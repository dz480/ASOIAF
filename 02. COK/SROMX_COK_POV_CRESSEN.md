# 1. Narrative Context

Following **Robert Baratheon's** death, rival claimants compete for the **Iron Throne**. On **Dragonstone**, his brother **Stannis** has declared himself king but commands too few men to seize **King's Landing**. The ageing **Maester Cressen**, who helped raise the Baratheon brothers, finds his counsel increasingly displaced by the red priestess **Melisandre**. A blazing comet hangs over the island as a white raven announces the end of the long summer.

# 4. Narrative Overview

At dawn on **DRAGONSTONE**, the ageing **Maester Cressen** watches a red comet burning over the fortress. A white raven has arrived from the **Citadel**, announcing that the long summer has ended. **Pylos**, the younger maester sent to assist him, brings **Shireen Baratheon** and **Patchface** to see the remarkable bird. Shireen fears the carved dragons of the castle, while Patchface's disjointed songs about shadows disturb her. Cressen recalls the shipwreck that killed the boys' father, **Steffon Baratheon**, and left the fool permanently changed.

Pylos then reports the return of **Davos Seaworth**, whom Cressen encounters on the painful climb towards the Chamber of the Painted Table. Davos has failed to persuade the storm lords to support **Stannis Baratheon**; many have joined his younger brother **Renly**. Stannis angrily rehearses the grievances of his inheritance and rejects Cressen's proposal to negotiate with the Starks and Arryns. His wife **Selyse** instead champions **Melisandre's** fiery predictions, including a vision of Renly dead. Horrified by the possibility of fratricide, Cressen is dismissed.

Back in his chambers, Cressen resolves to remove Melisandre. He takes crystals of **the strangler** from his medicines and conceals them in his sleeve before sleeping. He wakes after nightfall, finds that he has not been summoned and struggles to the feast in the **Great Hall**. Patchface knocks him over; Melisandre lifts him and crowns him with the fool's antlered helm before the laughing court. At the high table, Pylos now sits in Cressen's place, while Davos offers him a seat farther away.

When Selyse orders Cressen to wear the helm again, he notices Davos's half-filled cup. He slips a crystal into the wine and approaches Melisandre with an apparent toast to her god. She invites him to spill it, but he refuses. She drinks deeply and hands back what remains. Cressen drinks; the cup falls and shatters, and the poison closes his throat as Melisandre looks down at him.
