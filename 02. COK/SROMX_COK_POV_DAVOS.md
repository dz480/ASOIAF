# 1. Narrative Context

Following **Robert Baratheon**'s death, his brother **Stannis** prepares to claim the **Iron Throne** from **Dragonstone** with too little military support to challenge the larger factions. **Davos Seaworth**, formerly a smuggler from **Flea Bottom**, owes Stannis his knighthood, lands and livelihood, despite the finger joints Stannis took as punishment for past crimes. Davos has sons serving as ship captains, sailors and royal squires. He has also witnessed the death of **Maester Cressen**, who attempted to poison **Melisandre** but died from the poisoned wine himself. As the priestess gains authority over **Queen Selyse** and Stannis, Davos remains loyal to his king while questioning the faith and methods now taking hold at court.

# 4. Narrative Overview

At **Dragonstone**, **Davos Seaworth** watches the statues of the **Seven** burn before the castle gates. **Melisandre** proclaims **Stannis Baratheon** the reincarnated hero **Azor Ahai** as he pulls a sword from the flames, scorching his glove in the process. **Queen Selyse** and the red priestess's adherents acclaim **Lightbringer**, but Davos is disturbed by the destruction of familiar gods, the imprisonment of dissenters and the fate of **Maester Cressen**, whose attempted poisoning Davos recognised in the Prologue. His sons **Allard** and **Dale** disapprove of the burning and report that the queen's adherents have reserved the best supplies for themselves.

At a dockside inn, **Salladhor Saan** talks of the poorly defended capital and narrates the legend of Lightbringer's forging, including the sacrifice of **Nissa Nissa**. He and Davos disagree about the real power of the burning sword and the prospects of attacking King's Landing while rival armies remain active. That night Stannis summons Davos to the **Painted Table**, where **Maester Pylos** reads the proclamation accusing **Cersei** and **Jaime** of incest and declaring **Joffrey**, **Myrcella** and **Tommen** illegitimate. Stannis orders Davos and Dale to circulate copies along opposite coasts, while ravens carry the accusation inland. Alone with Davos, the king insists the claim is true but concedes he has little evidence that outsiders will accept. Davos warns against alienating subjects by invoking an unfamiliar god. Stannis remembers the weak hawk he once trained, **Proudwing**, and explains his willingness to try the powerful red priestess where older advisers and appeals have failed.

When Davos next appears, Stannis is besieging **Storm's End**. At a parley, its castellan **Ser Cortnay Penrose** refuses the king's terms because he will not surrender **Edric Storm**, Robert's bastard son. He challenges Stannis's nobles and offers a duel, but the king rejects a risk he has no military need to take. Riding away, Stannis questions Davos's loyalty and later asks his advice inside the royal pavilion. Davos counsels advancing against King's Landing and leaving the castle for later; Stannis instead insists on taking it and obtaining Edric. He declares that Melisandre has foreseen Penrose's death before another day passes and orders Davos to conduct her secretly beneath the castle.

That night Davos rows a small, black-sailed boat across **Shipbreaker Bay**. Sixteen years earlier he slipped through these waters bringing onions and other food to the besieged Stannis; now he believes he is delivering death. Melisandre disputes his distinction between good and evil and says older protective spells in Storm's End prevent a shadow from passing through its outer walls. Davos guides the boat through a hidden sea entrance at high tide and stops at an iron portcullis. There Melisandre reveals an immense pregnant belly, gives birth to a living black shadow and sends it through the bars. Davos recognises the shape as Stannis's. The chapter ends without witnessing the murder Penrose is predicted to suffer.

The next chapter begins in **Blackwater Bay**, with Davos commanding **Black Betha**, **Matthos** alongside him and **Allard** and **Dale** sailing their own galleys nearby. **Ser Imry Florent**, commander of the fleet, has dismissed Davos's proposal to send scouts ahead and drives the warships into the river in ordered lines. Davos notices small towers for a submerged chain and realises the defenders are drawing the attackers into an enclosed reach. He rams an enemy galley with Allard, then boards and captures **White Hart**, but wildfire spreads between entangled ships. A burning **Swordfish** rams a seemingly harmless hulk packed with jars of the substance. The explosion throws Davos from his deck into the water; the ship and his son Matthos disappear from his view. Struggling to swim clear, he sees the great chain pulled taut across the river mouth amid burning vessels and green flames. His survival beyond this moment, and the fates of his sons, remain unresolved within the selected Scope.
