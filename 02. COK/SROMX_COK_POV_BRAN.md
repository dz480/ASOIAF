# 1. Narrative Context

After **Eddard Stark**'s death and **Robb Stark**'s departure to wage war as **King in the North**, his younger brother **Bran Stark** remains at **Winterfell** as Robb's heir and the household's nominal prince. Bran's legs are paralysed after a fall whose circumstances he cannot fully remember; his lost freedom and his separation from his parents and siblings leave him resentful of confinement. **Maester Luwin** and **Ser Rodrik Cassel** administer the depleted castle, while **Hodor** carries Bran, **Osha** serves in the kitchens, and **Rickon** struggles with the family's dispersal. The Frey boys **Big Walder** and **Little Walder** have arrived as wards. Bran experiences increasingly vivid dreams of running as his direwolf **Summer**, and of a **three-eyed crow** demanding that he fly.

# 4. Narrative Overview

In **Winterfell**, **Bran Stark** finds the walls of his bedchamber oppressive and the howling of **Summer** and **Shaggydog** comforting. His brother **Robb** is fighting in the south, while **Rickon** clings to the household and its familiar faces. **Big Walder** and **Little Walder Frey**, fostered in Winterfell, have offended Bran by tormenting **Hodor**, excluding Bran from their rough games and provoking Shaggydog into attacking Little Walder. **Maester Luwin** and **Ser Rodrik Cassel** insist that he behave as his brother's heir, even while restricting his movements and confining the wolves to the godswood. After Bran refuses to settle at night, Luwin brings a sleeping draught. It cannot suppress the extraordinary wolf dream in which Bran sees and smells the godswood through Summer, longing for the freedom beyond its walls.

Visitors to the harvest feast bring the difficulties of northern government into the castle. Bran wears his finest clothes and receives them beside Rodrik and Luwin. **Wyman Manderly** requests authority to mint coins and build warships, while raising the question of recovering his captive son. The widowed **Lady Donella Hornwood** describes the threat posed by **Roose Bolton**'s bastard to her lands; **Mors Umber** offers himself as her husband, **Hother Umber** seeks coastal defences against wildling raiders, and **Leobald Tallhart** proposes a child heir drawn from his own family. Bran offers **Larence Snow**, the late Hornwood lord's bastard, as another possible successor, and Rodrik begins weighing the rival solutions. **Cley Cerwyn** arrives with rumours of **Stannis Baratheon**'s claim that **Joffrey** is Jaime and Cersei's son. The accusation causes Bran a sudden, bodily panic. That night his nightmare of the **three-eyed crow** also includes a golden man throwing him from a tower, a disturbing image that approaches a memory he cannot accept.

During the feast Bran rides **Dancer** along the Great Hall and receives the acclaim of the northern household. The feasting recalls a vanished family, but two new guests arrive: **Meera Reed** and her brother **Jojen**, children of the late **Eddard Stark**'s friend **Howland Reed**. They renew Greywater Watch's loyalty and ask about the wolves. That night Bran dreams that he is Summer in the godswood; the Reeds approach Summer and Shaggydog with neither fear nor surprise. Jojen touches the wolf's muzzle, and Bran experiences a sudden sensation of falling out of the dream. The siblings remain in Winterfell after the other guests depart and become the companions Bran prefers to the Freys.

In the godswood **Meera** plays at fighting Summer with her net and frog spear, while **Jojen** tells Bran of his green dreams, the third eye and a winged wolf bound down by stone chains. He claims Bran himself is the wolf and that the three-eyed crow sent the Reeds to free him. Bran resists his questions about the fall and grows so angry that Summer and Shaggydog advance on the visitors. Meera climbs a tree to save herself and Jojen; **Hodor** drives away the wolves. Bran asks **Luwin** about greenseers and magic. The maester acknowledges having studied the higher mysteries, but insists that old magical powers have passed away. Meera counters with another warning from Jojen: a strange supper in which Bran receives fresh, bloody meat while the Frey wards receive grey, dead meat.

A letter from Robb reports his victory over a Lannister army at **Oxcross**, the death of **Stafford Lannister**, and **Stevron Frey**'s death from a wound that had initially appeared slight. The Frey boys show interest in their family's succession rather than grief. Bran recognises the connection to Jojen's dream and asks him what danger is coming. Jojen describes black seawater flooding Winterfell and seeing **Alebelly**, **Mikken** and **Septon Chayle** drowned. Bran attempts to warn them, but they treat the prospect as impossible or laughable. Rodrik returns from the Hornwood lands with a prisoner called **Reek** and reports that Lady Hornwood was imprisoned and starved after being forced to wed the Bolton bastard. Rodrik believes the bastard killed, but his account remains a report, not an enacted scene from Bran's chapters. Jojen now tells Bran he also dreamed that the man called Reek would stand over Bran and Rickon's apparent bodies. Fear of the future collides with Bran's increasing awareness that his dreams belong to something beyond ordinary sleep.

Ironborn attacks on **Torrhen's Square** draw Rodrik and almost all available soldiers away. As Bran dreams through Summer one rainy night, unfamiliar men slip into Winterfell. The wolf's heightened senses detect the incursion, but Summer cannot escape the godswood to stop it. Bran wakes to find **Theon Greyjoy** in his bedchamber, proclaiming himself a prince and Winterfell's new master. Theon demands Bran's surrender, saying that obedience is the price of his people's safety. **Luwin**, bloodied in the defence, confirms **Alebelly** has been killed and a warning raven sent. Theon gathers the household in the Great Hall, takes the Stark high seat, and forces Bran to order his people to submit. **Mikken** rejects the invader and is run through with a spear; **Hodor** is beaten for crying out. Reek, released from the dungeons, offers his service to Theon. **Osha**, too, requests arms and swears obedience. Bran watches their apparent desertions in helpless bewilderment.

A later escape, the intervening search for the Stark boys and Winterfell's eventual sack take place outside the direct enactment of Bran's selected chapters. By his next chapter, Bran and Rickon are already hiding with Osha, Hodor and the Reeds in the **crypts**, beneath the empty tomb intended for their father. They have stayed concealed while pursuers are led to believe the boys fled into the countryside. Bran can now enter Summer's consciousness deliberately, but remains there for so long that **Jojen** fears his human body will starve. Through Summer he watches Winterfell burn, sees the corpses and scavengers in the wreckage and senses the devastation of the castle. Returning to his body after 3 days, he convinces the group to emerge.

They find shattered towers, burned stables, dead household men and bodies bearing both ironborn and **Dreadfort** affiliations. **Poxy Tym** is among the slain. The surviving direwolves find the fugitives, and **Summer** leads them to **Maester Luwin**, mortally wounded beneath the heart tree. Luwin recognises that the bodies presented as the Stark boys were impostors, since their legs lacked Bran's wasting; he explains that **Rodrik**, **Cley Cerwyn** and **Leobald Tallhart** are dead, and warns that the heirs must travel separately. Osha stays with the dying maester long enough to grant his final request before returning to the children. She takes Rickon and Shaggydog toward the kingsroad; Bran leaves with **Hodor**, **Meera**, **Jojen** and Summer by the Hunter's Gate. As Winterfell recedes, he sees that its stone walls and ancient roots still stand. Jojen says their road leads north.
