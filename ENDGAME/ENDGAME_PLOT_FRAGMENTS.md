# ASOIAF Endgame | Loose Plot-Point Register

**Status:** OPEN / PROVISIONAL / UNCOMMITTED  
**Established:** 14 Oct 2026  
**Scope:** Possible events after *A Feast for Crows* (AFFC) and *A Dance with Dragons* (ADWD), principally in *The Winds of Winter* (TWOW) and *A Dream of Spring* (ADOS).  
**Repository role:** Independent endgame-theory notebook, outside the authoritative ROM reading records.  
**Next intended phase:** Connect individual ideas to the unresolved macro-narrative threads (MNTs) identified in the completed AFFC and ADWD analyses.

> **Spoilers:** This file openly discusses the published novels, selected *Game of Thrones* television developments, and speculative endings.  
> **Interpretive boundary:** These are the reader's *possible* plot points, not a fixed outline, verified predictions, canonical events, or a claim to original authorship. Much of the eventual reconstruction may incorporate long-circulating fan theories and selected television endgame details. Precise provenance and textual support will be documented during later study.

## Purpose

Collect loose endgame ideas **now**, without committing to a particular outcome, chronology, or overarching solution. After completing and studying the existing books' MNTs, especially those left unresolved by AFFC and ADWD, assess which ideas fit Martin's established narrative conditions and which must be revised, connected, rejected, or left open.

The eventual project is **not a replacement fanfiction**. It is a personal attempt to infer where GRRM's unfinished novels are heading by combining textual evidence, existing theories, television-derived signals, and independent interpretation, with their status and provenance made clear.

## Provisional narrative fragments

The numbered IDs are stable handles for later discussion and MNT linking. Their numbering is **not** a locked chapter order or a claim of causal certainty.

### A. King's Landing, legitimacy, Aegon and Daenerys

**E01 — Cersei loses King's Landing to a popular revolt.**  
Cersei is overthrown by rebellion among the city's people. Her removal creates an opening for Aegon's claim.

**E02 — Aegon acquires the Iron Throne and broad support.**  
Before Daenerys arrives, Aegon takes the throne and secures the support of much of Westeros. His apparent legitimacy makes Daenerys's claim politically difficult even if she possesses dragons.

**E03 — Daenerys confronts Aegon.**  
Daenerys arrives at King's Landing and attacks the rival claimant with her dragons. Aegon is destroyed in the confrontation.

**E04 — Aerys's hidden wildfire turns conquest into catastrophe.**  
Dragonfire inadvertently ignites wildfire left beneath King's Landing by Daenerys's father. The resulting destruction reduces much of the city to ashes. The extent to which Daenerys anticipates or could prevent the catastrophe is an open question.

**E05 — Daenerys becomes reviled and disillusioned.**  
Survivors remember or portray her as a mad usurper. Whatever her intentions, she is held responsible for the city's destruction and rejected by the realm she intended to reclaim. She becomes disillusioned.

### B. Euron, Victarion, Dragonbinder and the Wall

**E06 — Euron intercepts Daenerys's Westeros-bound fleet.**  
During her voyage west, Euron attacks the fleet and appears to kill one of Daenerys's dragons. He withdraws. The dragon's identity, the attack's practical mechanism and its timing relative to Aegon's advance remain unresolved.

**E07 — The apparently killed dragon is alive and bound.**  
Daenerys later learns that Euron has the wounded dragon alive and under his control. Victarion is involved in sounding Dragonbinder, allowing Euron to bind the dragon. The mechanics of the horn and the costs to the blower require careful testing against the published text.

**E08 — Euron brings the apocalypse south.**  
Euron takes the bound dragon north and uses it to destroy the Wall, allowing the Others to advance into Westeros. This is a deliberate step in his desired catastrophe, rather than a mere accidental consequence of his ambition.

**E09 — Daenerys goes north after the dragon.**  
Learning that her lost dragon is captive, Daenerys abandons the ruined southern contest and travels north to reclaim her child. Her personal motive becomes intertwined with the war against the Others.

### C. Winterfell, Jon, Daenerys and the Others

**E10 — A resurrected Jon leads the defence of Winterfell.**  
Jon returns from his assassination and assumes a major leadership role in organising or commanding Winterfell's resistance to the Others. The conditions and consequences of his resurrection remain to be established.

**E11 — Jon and Daenerys develop a consequential relationship.**  
They meet and enter some relationship of cooperation, opposition, or shifting alignment. A romantic pairing is **not** a preferred assumption. The timing of their first encounter, motivations and political relationship remain unassigned.

**E12 — Jon confronts Euron and the enslaved dragon.**  
Jon rides another of Daenerys's dragons and kills Euron and the dragon that remains bound to him. The enslaved dragon cannot escape its magical bond. The identities and precise order of the surviving dragons remain open.

**E13 — Daenerys ends the threat of the Others and dies.**  
Daenerys, rather than Arya killing a single central antagonist, is pivotal to defeating the Others. She dies in the process. The actual supernatural mechanism of the victory is **unresolved**; the novels have not established the television show's single-point Night King solution.

**E14 — Two histories of Daenerys survive.**  
The southern realm, largely unaware of what happened in the far north, continues to remember Daenerys as a destructive usurper. Northern witnesses recognise her final sacrifice as an act that saved the realm. Her sacrifice does not simply erase the human cost of King's Landing.

### D. The Stark and royal endgames

**E15 — Jon rejects the Iron Throne and travels beyond the Wall.**  
Jon's parentage as the son of Rhaegar Targaryen and Lyanna Stark becomes consequential. He declines the crown and journeys beyond the former Wall to explore the far north after the Others are gone. The legal and political mechanism by which his claim might arise remains open.

**E16 — Bran becomes king and rebuilds the capital.**  
Bran takes the throne in Jon's place and presides over rebuilding King's Landing from ashes. As the living repository of Westerosi history, he may possess knowledge or motives beyond what others can understand. The final atmosphere is quietly sinister or ambiguous: any troubling intent remains **unconfirmed**, and the reader is left to interpret it.

## Provisional order and known gaps

A possible **partial** sequence, offered only for orientation:

1. Cersei is overthrown; Aegon becomes a popular claimant and takes the throne.
2. On Daenerys's westward journey, Euron apparently kills a dragon and escapes.
3. Daenerys reaches King's Landing; her attack on Aegon inadvertently ignites Aerys's wildfire; she is blamed.
4. Euron's possession of the living dragon becomes known, and he uses it to destroy the Wall.
5. A resurrected Jon organises the northern defence; Daenerys travels north and encounters him.
6. Jon confronts Euron and the enslaved dragon; Daenerys defeats the Others and dies.
7. The North and South preserve incompatible memories of Daenerys; Jon refuses the throne; Bran rules from a devastated capital.

**Not yet established:** The order of Jon's resurrection and Daenerys's southern campaign; the precise timing of the Winterfell defence; how Jon and Daenerys first meet; how Dragonbinder passes between Victarion and Euron; how defeating the Others works; who selects or recognises Bran; which additional factions and POV narratives drive these events.

## Future evaluation questions

- What has Martin *actually established*, and what comes only from television or fandom?
- Which political actors, troop movements, logistics and alliances could plausibly produce each outcome?
- Which character decisions follow established psychology rather than the needs of the proposed ending?
- Can Euron, Victarion and Dragonbinder plausibly produce the hypothesised dragon-capture and Wall-destruction sequence?
- What do the text's rules of magic, greenseeing, resurrection and the Others permit?
- Could Bran's eventual rule emerge from intelligible political circumstances, rather than succession by narrative convenience?
- Which threads and characters omitted from these fragments need independent resolutions?
- Where multiple theories compete, can uncertainty remain explicit instead of being concealed by a preferred answer?

## Working principles

1. **Collect before reconciling.** New loose points can be added without rewriting the entire model.
2. **Preserve provenance.** Mark a borrowed fan theory, television inspiration, textual inference or personal synthesis when its origin is known; add precise citations later.
3. **Keep certainty honest.** A compelling scenario remains a hypothesis until it survives scrutiny against the novel MNTs.
4. **Respect literary causality.** Evaluate politics, institutions, character psychology, foreshadowing and magical constraints alongside plot resemblance.
5. **Revise freely.** Entries may be amended, split, merged, contradicted or discarded. Nothing in this file constitutes a final reconstruction.
